Spreadsheets are where most job searches start, and where plenty of them quietly stop being updated. The columns multiply, the statuses drift, and after a few weeks you can't tell what needs attention today.

You don't need to record much. You need to record the right things, and to look at them regularly.

## Record only what you'll use

For each application, these are enough:

- **The role and company**, with a link to the posting, because postings disappear.
- **Where it stands:** saved, applied, interviewing, offer or closed.
- **The date you applied**, so you know when it has gone quiet.
- **The next action and its date.** This is the field that does the most work.
- **Which CV you sent** and **where you found the role**, so you can see what's working later.

If a detail wouldn't change what you do next, you can leave it out.

## Always have a next step

An application with no next step is one you've stopped managing. When you apply, set a date to follow up. A week is a sensible default for most roles. When you finish an interview, note what was promised and when to check in.

Your list then answers the question that matters each morning: what do I need to do today?

## Close things properly

Silence is an answer too. When a role has gone quiet for long enough, mark it closed and record why: rejected, no response, withdrew or declined. A shorter, honest list is easier to work from, and the reasons become useful information.

## Review once a week

Set aside fifteen minutes each week to ask three questions:

1. Which applications need a follow-up?
2. Which sources and CV versions are actually getting responses?
3. Am I applying at a pace I can keep up?

Look for patterns rather than single results. One reply proves little, but a source that keeps producing interviews deserves more of your time.
